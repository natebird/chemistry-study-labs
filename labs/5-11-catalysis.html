2. Key ideas

How it works

A new path, not a lower hill

A catalyst does not push down the original barrier. It provides a different mechanism whose highest barrier is lower, and/or it increases the number of effective collisions, for example by holding reactants in a good orientation.

Used and regenerated

Net amount unchanged

The catalyst is often consumed in the rate-determining step and regenerated in a later step, so its net concentration stays constant. Because it can be a reactant in the slow step, it can appear in the rate law.

Same start and finish

ΔH does not change

Reactants and products are the same with or without the catalyst, so ΔH is the same. Only the path between them, and the activation energy, changes. The forward and reverse barriers drop by the same amount.

Binding

Catalyst–reactant intermediates

Many catalysts, including most enzymes, bind the reactant to form a new intermediate (like an enzyme–substrate complex) that is oriented well or reacts with a lower barrier.

Acid–base catalysis

Proton on, proton off

An acid catalyst donates H+ to a reactant (or a base removes one), creating a new, more reactive intermediate. The proton is returned in a later step.

Surface catalysis

Bonding to a surface

Reactants bind to (adsorb on) a solid surface, such as nickel or platinum, where bonds like H–H are weakened or broken. New bound intermediates react on the surface, and the product leaves, freeing the site.

4. Catalysis on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Say the catalyst provides an alternative reaction pathway (a different mechanism) with a lower activation energy.
  • Connect to collisions: with a lower Ea, a greater fraction of collisions have enough energy, so the rate increases.
  • Identify a catalyst in a mechanism by its pattern: consumed in an early step and regenerated in a later step, absent from the overall equation.
  • When sketching, keep the reactant and product levels the same for both paths and make the catalyzed path's highest barrier lower. It may have more peaks.
  • For surface or enzyme catalysis, describe the reactant binding to the catalyst to form an intermediate.
  • If a catalyst appears in the rate law, explain that it is a reactant in the rate-determining step even though it is regenerated.

Common mistakes

  • Saying a catalyst "lowers the activation energy of the reaction" without mentioning the new pathway or mechanism.
  • Saying a catalyst changes ΔH, or drawing the catalyzed products at a different level.
  • Saying a catalyst is not used up "because it does not react." It does react; it is regenerated.
  • Confusing a catalyst with an intermediate: catalysts are there at the start, intermediates are not.
  • Claiming a catalyst gives the particles more kinetic energy or raises the temperature.
  • Thinking a catalyst only speeds up the forward reaction. The reverse barrier is lowered by the same amount.
