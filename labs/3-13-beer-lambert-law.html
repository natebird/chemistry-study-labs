2. Key ideas

A = εbc

The Beer–Lambert law

Absorbance A has no units. ε is the molar absorptivity (M−1 cm−1), a property of the substance at a given wavelength. b is the path length in cm (usually 1.00 cm). c is the molar concentration.

Direct proportion

Double c, double A

At a fixed wavelength and path length, absorbance is directly proportional to concentration. A plot of A against c for standards is a straight line through (or very near) the origin: a calibration curve.

λmax

Measure at the absorbance maximum

At λmax, ε is largest, so a small change in concentration gives the largest change in absorbance. The method is most sensitive there, and small wavelength drifts matter least because the peak is flat on top.

Color

Absorbed vs. seen

A red solution looks red because it transmits red light and absorbs green-blue light. Its λmax is in the green-blue region, near 500 nm, not at a red wavelength.

Blank

Zero the instrument

Before measuring, a cuvette of the solvent (the blank) is used to set A = 0. Then the reading reflects only the absorbing solute, not the solvent or the glass.

Error analysis

Follow the absorbance

Any error that makes the measured absorbance too high makes the calculated concentration too high, and the reverse. Ask: did this change A for the unknown only, or for the standards too?

4. Answering it on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Solve A = εbc for the unknown quantity and show the substitution with units: c = A ÷ (εb).
  • From a calibration line A = mc + k, solve c = (A − k) ÷ m; the slope m equals εb.
  • Justify the wavelength: "at λmax the absorbance is greatest, so the measurement is most sensitive to changes in concentration."
  • For error questions, state the effect on the measured absorbance and then the effect on the calculated concentration.
  • Connect color to the absorbed wavelength: the solution absorbs the complementary color of what you see.
  • Say why a blank is used: it sets A = 0 for everything except the solute.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a wavelength the solution transmits (red light for a red solution). It barely absorbs there.
  • Giving A units. Absorbance is unitless; ε carries M−1 cm−1.
  • Forgetting that b and ε cancel out of an error if the standards and unknown share them.
  • Saying a diluted sample gives a "higher concentration because more light gets through." More light through means lower A and lower calculated c.
  • Extrapolating far past the highest standard.
  • Confusing %T with A: A = 1 means only 10% of the light gets through, not that it all does.
