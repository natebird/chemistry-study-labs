Properties of the Equilibrium Constant Study Lab

When you change how a reaction is written, its equilibrium constant changes in a predictable way: reverse the reaction and K is inverted, multiply the coefficients by a factor and K is raised to that power, add reactions together and their K values multiply. Use those three rules to build a target reaction from given ones, the way a multistep process is described by one overall K.

2. Key ideas

Reverse

K becomes 1/K

Writing a reaction backward swaps the numerator and denominator of its expression, so Kreverse = 1/Kforward. A large K becomes a small one, and the reverse of a reaction with K = 1 still has K = 1.

Scale

K becomes Kn

Multiplying every coefficient by n multiplies every exponent in the expression by n, so the new K is Kn. Doubling squares K; halving takes the square root. Not K × n.

Add

K values multiply

When reactions are added, the overall expression is the product of the individual expressions (species that appear on both sides cancel), so Koverall = K1 × K2. This is how a multistep process gets one overall K.

Combine

All three at once

In general, if overall = n1(reaction 1) + n2(reaction 2), then K = K1n1 × K2n2. A negative n means that reaction was reversed.

Q too

Same algebra for Q

Q and K have the same mathematical form, so every one of these manipulations also applies to Q. Reversing a reaction inverts Q; adding reactions multiplies their Q values.

Compare with Hess's law

Add ΔH, multiply K

The same equation steps are used for ΔH in Unit 6, but the math is different: ΔH changes sign where K is inverted, is multiplied by n where K is raised to the power n, and ΔH values add where K values multiply.

4. Properties of K on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Show how the given equations combine into the target: which one is reversed, which is doubled, and that intermediates cancel.
  • Write the K relationship before the numbers: "K = K1 × (1/K2)" or "K = (K1)1/2."
  • Then substitute and report K without units, with sensible significant figures.
  • For a multistep process, state that the overall K is the product of the K values of the steps.
  • Check the size of your answer: reversing a large-K reaction must give a small K.
  • When asked about Q, apply exactly the same manipulations as for K.

Common mistakes

  • Multiplying K by the factor (2K) instead of raising it to the power (K2).
  • Adding K values when reactions are added. Add ΔH; multiply K.
  • Making K negative when a reaction is reversed. It becomes the reciprocal, never negative.
  • Forgetting to apply the power to a reversed reaction that is also scaled: reversing and doubling gives K−2, not 1/(2K).
  • Leaving an intermediate uncancelled, so the combined equation does not match the target.
  • Mixing up which given K belongs to which reaction after reordering them.
