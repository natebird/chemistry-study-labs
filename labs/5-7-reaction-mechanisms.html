2. Key ideas

Definition

A mechanism is a sequence of steps

Each step is an elementary reaction: it happens in one collision (or one molecule falling apart) exactly as written. A mechanism can include reactants, intermediates, products and catalysts.

The first test

Steps must add up

Write the steps one under another, add everything on the left and everything on the right, and cancel any species that appears on both sides. What is left must be the overall balanced equation. If it is not, the mechanism is wrong.

Made, then used

Intermediate

An intermediate is produced in an earlier step and consumed in a later one. It cancels out of the sum, so it never appears in the overall equation, and it exists only while the reaction is going on.

Used, then given back

Catalyst

A catalyst is consumed in an earlier step and regenerated in a later one. It also cancels out of the sum, but it is present at the start and is still there at the end. Catalysts are covered in depth in topic 5.11.

Counting particles

Molecularity

The number of reactant particles that collide in an elementary step: one (unimolecular), two (bimolecular) or three (termolecular, rare because three particles seldom collide at once). Only elementary steps have a molecularity.

Evidence

Detecting an intermediate

Finding a proposed intermediate in the reaction mixture is evidence for a mechanism that includes it over one that does not. It supports the mechanism; it does not prove it. The CED states that collecting the data used to detect an intermediate is not assessed.

4. Mechanisms on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • When asked for the overall equation, add the steps and show which species cancel. Multiply a step first if the mechanism says it happens twice.
  • Identify an intermediate with a reason: "NO3 is produced in step 1 and consumed in step 2, so it does not appear in the overall equation."
  • Identify a catalyst with a reason: "I− is consumed in step 1 and regenerated in step 2."
  • When judging a mechanism, check first that the steps add up to the balanced equation (atoms and charge).
  • For evidence questions, say that detecting the intermediate supports the mechanism that contains it, and explain why the other mechanism cannot account for it.
  • Name the molecularity of a step from its reactant side: 2NO + H2 is termolecular.

Common mistakes

  • Calling any species that appears in two steps an intermediate. NO2 appears in both steps of the NO2 + CO mechanism, but more is used than made, so it is a reactant.
  • Mixing up intermediates and catalysts. Order matters: made then used is an intermediate; used then remade is a catalyst.
  • Leaving an intermediate in the overall equation, or writing the overall equation without canceling.
  • Writing the overall rate law from the overall coefficients. That only works for an elementary reaction (topic 5.8 shows how to get it from a mechanism).
  • Saying that detecting an intermediate proves a mechanism. It is evidence, not proof.
  • Thinking an intermediate is the same as a transition state. An intermediate is a real, if short-lived, species in a valley; a transition state is the peak of a single step.
