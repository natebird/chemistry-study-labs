Calculating Equilibrium Concentrations Study Lab

If you know the balanced equation, the starting amounts and K, you can predict every equilibrium concentration or partial pressure. The route is always the same: compare Q with K to find the direction, write an ICE table in terms of one unknown x, substitute into the K expression, and solve. Here you set the starting mixture and K, and the lab builds the table, picks the algebra, and checks the answer.

2. Key ideas

Step 1

Q tells you the direction

Calculate Q from the starting amounts. If Q < K, the reaction runs forward: reactants are consumed and products form. If Q > K, it runs in reverse. If Q = K, nothing changes overall, because the forward and reverse rates are already equal.

Step 2

One unknown, many species

Every change is tied to the same x through the coefficients. For 2HI ⇌ H2 + I2, if H2 changes by +x, HI changes by −2x. The signs come from the direction; the multipliers come from the balanced equation.

Step 3

Substitute the E row

Only equilibrium amounts go into the K expression. Write each one from the E row, raise it to its coefficient, and set the result equal to K. Pure solids and liquids still do not appear.

Step 4

Pick the algebra

If both sides are perfect squares, take the square root. If K is small and x will be tiny next to the starting amount, drop x from (C − x) and check that x is under 5% of C. Otherwise rearrange to a quadratic and keep the root that gives no negative amounts.

Units

Match the amounts to K

Kc takes molar concentrations; Kp takes partial pressures (usually in atm). Change moles to molarity before the ICE table if you are given moles and a volume. Change values can be in M or atm, but never mix them in one table.

Step 5

Check by substituting back

Put your equilibrium amounts back into the expression. You should get K (to rounding). A negative concentration or a value of Q on the wrong side of K means a sign or root was chosen wrongly.

4. Equilibrium concentrations on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Write the K expression first, then the ICE table with initial, change and equilibrium rows labeled.
  • Justify the direction with numbers: "Q = 0.25, which is less than K = 4.0, so the reaction proceeds to the right."
  • Show the substituted equation, such as 0.15 = (2x)2/(1.00 − x), before solving.
  • If you use the small-x approximation, say so and show the 5% check.
  • Report the quantity asked for, which is often 2x or C − x, not x itself, with units (M or atm).
  • A quick plug-back check that Q at equilibrium equals K catches most sign errors.

Common mistakes

  • Reporting x as the answer when the question asks for [HI] = 2x or [PCl5] = 0.200 − x.
  • Forgetting to square (2x): writing 2x2 instead of 4x2.
  • Assuming the reaction runs forward when products are present at the start. Check Q first.
  • Using moles in the Kc expression without dividing by the volume.
  • Keeping the small-x approximation when x turns out to be a large fraction of the starting amount.
  • Keeping the negative root of the quadratic, or a root larger than the starting amount, which would give a negative concentration.
