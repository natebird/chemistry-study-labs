2. Key ideas

Definition

Entropy is dispersal

Entropy measures how many ways a system's particles and their energy can be arranged. When matter spreads into more space, or energy spreads over more possible states, there are more arrangements and S goes up. "Disorder" is a loose shortcut; on the exam, argue in terms of dispersal of matter and energy.

Phase

Solid < liquid < gas

Particles in a solid only vibrate in place. In a liquid they slide past one another. In a gas they move freely through the whole container. So melting, vaporizing, and subliming all have ΔS > 0; freezing, condensing, and deposition have ΔS < 0. The jump from liquid to gas is much larger than from solid to liquid.

Gas moles

Count moles of gas

Gases dominate entropy. If a reaction makes more moles of gas than it uses, ΔS > 0; fewer moles of gas, ΔS < 0. When the gas moles are equal, the sign can't be predicted from the equation alone and ΔS is usually small.

Volume

More room, more entropy

A gas that expands at constant temperature has more positions available to each molecule, so its matter is more dispersed and S increases. Compressing a gas lowers S.

Temperature

Hotter means more spread-out energy

By kinetic molecular theory, raising the temperature widens the distribution of molecular kinetic energies. Energy is spread across more energy states, so entropy increases with temperature even if nothing else changes.

Units

Joules, not kilojoules

Entropy changes are reported in J/(mol·K), or J/K for a reaction as written. Enthalpy is in kJ. Keep that difference in mind for topic 9.3, where ΔS must be converted to kJ before it is combined with ΔH.

Look for the change that disperses the most matter. Gas-phase changes usually win over everything else.

4. Entropy on the FRQ

What earns points

  • State the sign of ΔS and give a particle-level reason in the same sentence: "ΔS > 0 because 1 mol of gas is produced from solids, so matter becomes more dispersed."
  • For reactions, count the moles of gas on each side and say which side has more.
  • For phase changes, name the phases and describe the change in particle freedom or volume.
  • For a temperature change, refer to the distribution of kinetic energies broadening (energy dispersal).
  • For a gas expanding at constant temperature, say the molecules can occupy a larger volume.
  • Use the words "dispersed" or "more possible arrangements"; "more random" alone may not earn the point.

Common mistakes

  • Counting all molecules instead of only gas molecules. Two moles of a solid do not outweigh one mole of a gas.
  • Deciding the sign of ΔS from ΔH. An exothermic reaction can have ΔS of either sign.
  • Claiming a sign when the moles of gas are equal. Say the change is small and cannot be predicted from the equation alone.
  • Saying a gas that expands at constant temperature has faster molecules. Its speed distribution is unchanged; only the volume grew.
  • Treating "thermodynamically favored" as the same as "ΔS > 0". Favorability depends on ΔG°, which includes ΔH° (topic 9.3).
  • Writing entropy units as kJ/(mol·K). Tabulated entropies are in J/(mol·K).
