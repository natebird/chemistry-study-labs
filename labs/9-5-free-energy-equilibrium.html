ΔG° and K are two ways of saying the same thing: ΔG° = −RT ln K. A negative ΔG° means K > 1 and products are favored at equilibrium; a positive ΔG° means K < 1. Because the relationship is exponential, a few kJ/mol change K by a factor of ten. Convert in both directions below and build a feel for the sizes.

2. Key ideas

The equation

ΔG° = −RT ln K

Rearranged, K = e−ΔG°/RT. The equations sheet gives ΔG° = −RT ln K; the rearranged form is in the CED, and you can get it by solving for K. R = 8.314 J/(mol·K), T in kelvins, and ΔG° must be in joules per mole to match R. Converting kJ to J is the step most often missed.

Signs

Favored means K > 1

ΔG° < 0 ⇔ K > 1: products favored at equilibrium. ΔG° > 0 ⇔ K < 1: reactants favored. ΔG° = 0 ⇔ K = 1. "Thermodynamically favored" is the same claim as "K > 1 under standard conditions."

Estimation

Compare ΔG° with RT

At 298 K, RT = 2.48 kJ/mol. If |ΔG°| is about RT or smaller, K is close to 1 and both sides are present in comparable amounts. If |ΔG°| is many times RT, K is far from 1. You can reason this way without a calculator.

Exponential

5.7 kJ/mol per factor of 10

At 298 K, every 5.7 kJ/mol of ΔG° (2.303RT) changes K by a factor of 10. So ΔG° = −11.4 kJ/mol gives K ≈ 100, and ΔG° = −57 kJ/mol gives K ≈ 1010. Small energy differences make huge differences in K.

Not K = 0

K < 1 still means some product

ΔG° > 0 does not mean "no reaction." It means the equilibrium lies toward reactants. Acetic acid (ΔG° ≈ +27 kJ/mol for ionization) still ionizes a little, which is why vinegar is acidic.

Any K

Works for every type of K

The same equation links ΔG° to Ka, Kb, Ksp, Kw, and Kp. For dissolving AgCl, ΔG° = +55.7 kJ/mol gives Ksp ≈ 1.7 × 10−10.

4. ΔG° and K on the FRQ

What earns points

  • Write ΔG° = −RT ln K, convert ΔG° to J/mol, and show the substitution with R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) and T in K.
  • Report K without units, with a sensible number of significant figures; report ΔG° with sign and kJ/mol.
  • For a qualitative question, connect the sign: "ΔG° < 0, so K > 1 and products are favored at equilibrium."
  • When asked to estimate, compare |ΔG°| with RT ≈ 2.5 kJ/mol at 298 K to decide whether K is close to 1.
  • Use the K that matches the process: Ksp for dissolving, Ka for acid ionization, Kp for gases.
  • If K is given and ΔG° asked, use ln, not log: ln K = 2.303 log K.

Common mistakes

  • Using ΔG° in kJ with R = 8.314 J/(mol·K). The exponent comes out a thousand times too small and K ≈ 1.
  • Dropping the negative sign: K = e+ΔG°/RT gives 1/K.
  • Using log instead of ln, which gives a ΔG° 2.303 times too small.
  • Using °C for T.
  • Saying ΔG° > 0 means no products at all. It means K < 1.
  • Confusing K with the rate. A huge K says nothing about how fast equilibrium is reached (topic 9.4).
