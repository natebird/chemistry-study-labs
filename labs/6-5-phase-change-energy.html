2. Key ideas

Flat segments

Constant temperature during a phase change

While a pure substance melts or boils, its temperature stays constant. The energy absorbed overcomes intermolecular attractions (raising potential energy) instead of raising the average kinetic energy, so the thermometer does not move until the phase change is complete.

Sloped segments

One phase warming or cooling

Between phase changes, energy changes the temperature of a single phase: q = mcΔT, with the specific heat of that phase. Ice, liquid water, and steam all have different specific heats, so the slopes differ.

Per mole

q = nΔH for a phase change

The energy for a phase change is the moles of substance times the molar enthalpy of that change: q = nΔHfus for melting, q = nΔHvap for boiling. Convert grams to moles first.

Reverse processes

Same size, opposite sign

Freezing releases exactly the energy melting absorbs, and condensing releases what vaporizing absorbs: ΔHfreezing = −ΔHfus and ΔHcondensation = −ΔHvap. A cooling curve is the heating curve run backward.

Why boiling is so long

ΔHvap ≫ ΔHfus

Melting only loosens the particles; they stay in contact in the liquid. Vaporizing separates them almost completely, overcoming nearly all the attractions. For water, ΔHvap (40.7 kJ/mol) is almost seven times ΔHfus (6.01 kJ/mol).

Endo or exo

Direction of energy flow

Melting, vaporization, and sublimation are endothermic: the system's energy increases. Freezing, condensation, and deposition are exothermic: the system's energy decreases and the surroundings warm.

4. Phase-change energy on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Split a multistep problem into segments and label each: warm the solid, melt, warm the liquid, boil, warm the gas.
  • Use q = mcΔT on slopes (grams) and q = nΔH on plateaus (moles). Show the gram-to-mole conversion.
  • Put every segment in the same unit (usually kJ) before adding them.
  • Explain a plateau at the particle level: "the energy absorbed overcomes intermolecular attractions, increasing potential energy while the average kinetic energy, and so the temperature, stays constant."
  • For condensing or freezing, give the energy a negative sign for the system and say it is released to the surroundings.
  • When reading a heating curve made with a constant heating rate, compare plateau lengths: longer plateau, larger molar enthalpy (for the same sample).

Common mistakes

  • Using q = mcΔT across a phase change. On a plateau ΔT = 0, which would wrongly give q = 0.
  • Multiplying grams by ΔH in kJ/mol. Convert to moles first.
  • Using liquid water's specific heat for ice or steam.
  • Adding J and kJ without converting.
  • Saying "temperature stays constant because no energy is absorbed." Energy is absorbed; it goes into overcoming attractions.
  • Saying bonds break when water boils. Covalent O–H bonds stay intact; only intermolecular attractions are overcome.
