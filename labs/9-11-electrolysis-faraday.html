2. Key ideas

Charge

I = q/t

Current is charge per time: 1 A = 1 C/s. The total charge passed is q = I × t, with t in seconds. This equation is on the AP equation sheet.

Faraday constant

96,485 C per mole of electrons

F converts charge into moles of electrons: mol e⁻ = q / F. From here on, it is ordinary stoichiometry.

The bridge

The half-reaction gives the ratio

Cu2+ + 2e⁻ → Cu means 2 mol e⁻ per mol Cu. Ag+ + e⁻ → Ag needs 1, Al3+ + 3e⁻ → Al needs 3. The charge on the ion sets how much metal each coulomb buys.

Electroplating

Mass on the cathode

The object to be plated is the cathode, where metal ions are reduced and deposited. The mass gained is moles of metal × molar mass. A metal anode of the same element dissolves, replacing the ions.

Run it backward

Time, current, or ion charge

The same chain works in reverse: grams → moles → mol e⁻ → coulombs → seconds (or amps). With I, t and the mass, you can find mol e⁻ per mol metal, which is the charge on the ion.

Series cells

Same electrons, different products

Cells wired in series carry the same current, so the same moles of electrons pass through each. The moles of product differ by the electrons each needs: 1 Ag, ½ Cu, ⅓ Al per mol e⁻.

The chain, forward and backward

  1. Convert time to seconds. q = I × t (C).
  2. mol e⁻ = q ÷ 96,485 C/mol e⁻.
  3. mol product = mol e⁻ ÷ (electrons in the half-reaction per product).
  4. mass = mol × molar mass; for a gas at STP, volume = mol × 22.4 L/mol.
  5. Backward: mass → mol → mol e⁻ (× electrons per product) → q (× F) → t = q ÷ I.

4. Faraday problems on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Write the half-reaction first and point to the electron coefficient you use.
  • Show the units at each step: (2.00 C/s)(1800 s) = 3600 C; 3600 C × (1 mol e⁻ / 96,485 C) = 0.0373 mol e⁻.
  • Carry the mole ratio as a factor: 0.0373 mol e⁻ × (1 mol Cu / 2 mol e⁻).
  • Finish with the quantity asked for (grams, liters, seconds, amps) and three significant figures when the data have three.
  • For "identify the ion" questions, compute mol e⁻ ÷ mol metal and round to a whole number, then state the charge.
  • In series circuits, say explicitly that the same charge passes through every cell.

Common mistakes

  • Using minutes or hours in q = It. The coulomb is an ampere-second.
  • Forgetting the electron ratio: treating Cu2+ as if one electron deposits one Cu atom doubles the answer.
  • Multiplying by the ion charge instead of dividing (or the reverse) when going from mol e⁻ to mol metal.
  • Thinking a higher concentration of metal ion deposits more metal. At a fixed current and time the electrons, not the ions, limit the amount.
  • Thinking a bigger cathode plates more mass. It spreads the same mass over more area.
  • Using 22.4 L/mol when the gas is not at STP. Use PV = nRT for other conditions.
