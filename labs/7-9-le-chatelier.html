Introduction to Le Châtelier’s Principle Study Lab

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, it shifts in the direction that partly counteracts the disturbance, and a new equilibrium is set up. Le Châtelier’s principle predicts which way: toward the side that uses up an added substance, replaces a removed one, absorbs added heat, or makes fewer gas particles when squeezed. Here you apply each kind of stress to six classic systems and see the shift in color, pH and concentration.

2. Key ideas

Concentration

Use up what was added

Adding a reactant or removing a product shifts the reaction right; adding a product or removing a reactant shifts it left. The shift only partly undoes the change: an added substance ends up above its old equilibrium value, a removed one below it.

Pure solids and liquids

Adding more does nothing

Pure solids and liquids do not appear in the K expression, so adding more CaCO3(s) to CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g) does not shift the equilibrium. Removing a solid has no effect either, as long as some remains. Adding water to an aqueous equilibrium is different: it dilutes the dissolved species (see Dilution).

Volume and pressure

Count the gas particles

Decreasing the volume of a gas mixture shifts it toward the side with fewer moles of gas; increasing the volume shifts it toward more. If both sides have the same number of moles of gas, there is no shift. Adding an inert gas at constant volume changes no partial pressure, so nothing shifts.

Dilution

The aqueous version

Adding water to an aqueous equilibrium lowers every dissolved concentration, and the system shifts toward the side with more dissolved particles. For Co(H2O)62+ + 4Cl− ⇌ CoCl42− + 6H2O, dilution turns the solution pink.

Temperature

Treat heat as a substance

For an endothermic reaction, heat is a reactant: heating shifts right. For an exothermic reaction, heat is a product: heating shifts left. Temperature is the only stress that changes the value of K.

Catalyst

Faster, not further

A catalyst lowers the activation energy for the forward and reverse reactions equally. Equilibrium is reached sooner, but the equilibrium amounts and K are unchanged.

4. Le Châtelier on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Name the direction (toward reactants or toward products) and tie it to the specific stress: "the added Cl− is partly consumed."
  • For volume changes, count moles of gas on each side and say which side has fewer.
  • For temperature, state whether the forward reaction is endothermic or exothermic, and say that K changes.
  • Connect the shift to what you would see or measure: color, pH, temperature, mass of precipitate, pressure.
  • For "no shift" answers, give the reason: solid not in the expression, equal moles of gas, catalyst affects both rates equally.
  • A justification using Q and K (topic 7.10) is often accepted and is more precise than "the system wants to."

Common mistakes

  • Saying the shift completely restores the original concentration. It only partly offsets the stress.
  • Shifting the reaction when a pure solid or liquid is added.
  • Thinking any increase in total pressure causes a shift. Adding an inert gas at constant volume does not.
  • Claiming a catalyst increases the yield at equilibrium.
  • Saying K changes when a concentration or the volume changes. Only temperature changes K.
  • Mixing up which way heat goes: ΔH > 0 means heat is absorbed in the forward direction.
