Lewis Diagrams Study Lab

A Lewis diagram shows every valence electron in a molecule or ion as a bond or a lone pair. A short, reliable procedure produces the diagram every time: count the electrons, connect the atoms, fill octets, then make multiple bonds if needed. 2. Key ideas

Step 1

Count valence electrons

Add the valence electrons of every atom (the group number for main-group elements: C 4, N 5, O 6, halogens 7). Add one electron for each negative charge on an ion; subtract one for each positive charge. Every one of these electrons must appear in the diagram, no more and no fewer.

Step 2

Connect the skeleton

The least electronegative atom (never H) usually goes in the center. H and F are always terminal, bonded to one atom. Connect each terminal atom to the center with a single bond (2 electrons each).

Steps 3 and 4

Fill octets, outside first

Give each terminal atom lone pairs until it has 8 electrons (H needs only 2). Put any electrons that are left on the central atom as lone pairs.

Step 5

Make multiple bonds if needed

If the central atom still has fewer than 8 electrons, move a lone pair from a neighboring atom into the bond to make a double bond (or two pairs for a triple). The electron total does not change; the pair is just shared now.

Fewer than 8

Incomplete octets

Boron (and beryllium) are stable with fewer than 8 electrons: B in BF3 has 6. Making a B=F double bond would give B an octet but put a positive formal charge on F, so the AP-preferred diagram keeps three single bonds.

More than 8

Expanded octets

Atoms in period 3 and below (P, S, Cl, Xe, I) can hold more than 8 electrons around the central atom, as in PCl5 (10), SF6 (12), and XeF4 (12). Period 2 atoms (C, N, O, F) never exceed 8.

4. Writing it on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Show every valence electron: every bond as a line and every lone pair as two dots. Missing lone pairs on terminal atoms is the most common lost point.
  • Check the total: bonds × 2 + lone pairs × 2 must equal the valence electron count.
  • Check every atom: 2 electrons for H, 8 for C, N, O, F; B may have 6; period 3 and beyond may have more than 8.
  • For an ion, draw square brackets around the whole diagram and write the charge outside the top-right bracket.
  • Put the least electronegative atom in the center (other than H), with H and F on the outside.
  • When asked to "justify," cite the counts: "C has four bonds (8 electrons) and each O has two bonds and two lone pairs (8 electrons)."

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting to add electrons for a negative charge (or subtract for a positive charge) on an ion.
  • Satisfying every octet but using too many electrons. Always compare to the total from step 1.
  • Giving H a lone pair or a double bond.
  • Giving C, N, O, or F more than 8 electrons. Only period 3 and later atoms can expand their octet.
  • Putting H or F in the center of the skeleton.
  • Making a multiple bond to an atom that already has an octet, or to boron in BF3.
