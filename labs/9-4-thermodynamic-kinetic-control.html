Thermodynamic and Kinetic Control Study Lab

ΔG° tells you which direction a reaction is favored. It says nothing about how fast it goes. Many favored reactions, like diamond turning into graphite or a sugar cube burning in air, have activation barriers so high that they do not happen at any measurable rate. Those reactions are under kinetic control. Explore both questions, "favored?" and "fast?", below.

2. Key ideas

Two questions

Favored is not the same as fast

Thermodynamics (ΔG°, K) compares the starting and ending states: which side is favored. Kinetics (rate, activation energy) describes the path between them: how fast you get there. A reaction can be favored and slow, unfavored and fast, or any other combination.

Definition

Kinetic control

A process that is thermodynamically favored but does not proceed at a measurable rate is under kinetic control. The usual reason is a high activation energy: at ordinary temperatures almost no collisions have enough energy to get over the barrier.

Not equilibrium

No change does not mean equilibrium

A mixture of H₂ and O₂ can sit unchanged for years. It is not at equilibrium; it is just reacting too slowly to notice. If a catalyst or a spark makes the reaction go and the composition changes a lot, the system was not at equilibrium before.

Catalysts

Catalysts change the path, not ΔG°

A catalyst provides a mechanism with a lower activation energy, so a kinetically controlled reaction can become fast. It lowers the barrier for the forward and reverse reactions equally, so ΔG°, K, and the equilibrium composition do not change.

Temperature

Heat or a spark gets it started

At higher temperature a larger fraction of collisions have enough energy to cross the barrier, so the rate increases sharply. A spark heats a small region; if the reaction is exothermic, the heat released keeps the reaction going through the rest of the mixture.

Unfavored and fast

Speed cannot beat ΔG° > 0

If ΔG° > 0, a low barrier only means the reaction reaches equilibrium quickly. At equilibrium, reactants still dominate because K < 1. A catalyst never makes an unfavored reaction produce more product.

4. Kinetic control on the FRQ

What earns points

  • When a favored reaction (ΔG° < 0 or K > 1) is not observed, say it is under kinetic control because its activation energy is high.
  • Explain the rate at the particle level: few collisions have energy greater than or equal to the activation energy.
  • Keep the two ideas separate in your wording: "thermodynamically favored" for ΔG°, "rate" or "slow" for kinetics.
  • State that a catalyst lowers the activation energy without changing ΔG° or K.
  • If no change is observed, do not claim equilibrium unless you have evidence that the forward and reverse rates are equal.
  • For a spark or heating, explain that it supplies the energy needed to overcome the activation barrier.

Common mistakes

  • Saying "ΔG° < 0, so the reaction happens quickly." ΔG° contains no information about rate.
  • Saying a reaction that shows no change must be at equilibrium.
  • Claiming a catalyst makes ΔG° more negative or shifts the equilibrium toward products.
  • Claiming a spark changes ΔG° or K. It only gets enough molecules over the barrier.
  • Calling a slow reaction "not spontaneous." The correct statement is "favored but kinetically slow."
  • Thinking a fast reaction must be favored. Speed and favorability are independent.
