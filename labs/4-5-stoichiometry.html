    2. Key ideas

    Moles are the hub

    Always go through moles

    Coefficients relate moles, not grams or liters. Convert every given amount to moles first: mass ÷ molar mass, molarity × volume in liters, or PV/RT for a gas. Convert back to the units asked for only at the end.

    Mole ratio

    The bridge between substances

    Moles of B = moles of A × (coefficient of B ÷ coefficient of A). This is the only step that uses the balanced equation, so the equation must be balanced before you start.

    Smallest n ÷ coefficient

    Limiting reactant

    Divide each reactant's moles by its coefficient. The smallest result is the limiting reactant: it runs out first and sets how much product forms. The reactant with the larger mass is not necessarily in excess.

    Leftovers

    Excess reactant

    The excess reactant left = moles you started with − moles used, where moles used = moles of limiting reactant × the mole ratio. Convert to grams if asked.

    Actual ÷ theoretical

    Percent yield

    Theoretical yield is the most product the limiting reactant can make. Percent yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100%. Below 100% points to losses or incomplete reaction; above 100% means the product is impure or still wet.

    PV = nRT and M = n/V

    Gas and solution stoichiometry

    For gases, n = PV/RT (T in kelvins); the reference sheet also gives 22.4 L/mol for an ideal gas at STP (273.15 K, 1.0 atm). For solutions, n = M × V with V in liters. Then use mole ratios as usual.

    4. Answering the FRQ

    What earns points on the FRQ

    • Show each conversion with units: "4.00 g H₂ × (1 mol / 2.016 g) = 1.98 mol H₂." Graders need to see the setup, not just the number.
    • Write the mole ratio explicitly, with both substance names: "× (2 mol H₂O / 2 mol H₂)."
    • When two reactant amounts are given, justify the limiting reactant: compare moles ÷ coefficients, or compute the product each reactant could make and take the smaller.
    • Report answers with the correct number of significant figures (usually set by the given data) and units.
    • Convert mL to L and °C to K before using M = n/V or PV = nRT.
    • For percent yield, divide actual by theoretical, not the other way around, and explain a value over 100% (impure or wet product).

    Common mistakes

    • Using grams in a mole ratio: "4 g H₂ makes 4 g H₂O" is wrong; coefficients relate moles.
    • Picking the limiting reactant by smallest mass or smallest mole count without dividing by the coefficients.
    • Flipping the mole ratio (multiplying by 3/2 instead of 2/3).
    • Using 22.4 L/mol at conditions other than STP. At other conditions, use PV = nRT.
    • Forgetting to subtract the amount used when asked how much excess reactant remains.
    • Forgetting the subscript in solution problems: 0.10 M CaCl₂ is 0.20 M in Cl⁻.
