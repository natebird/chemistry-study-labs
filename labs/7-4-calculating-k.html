2. Key ideas

The rule

Only equilibrium values go into K

K is calculated from concentrations or partial pressures measured at equilibrium. Initial amounts only help you find the equilibrium ones. Substituting initial values gives Q at time zero, not K.

Units first

Moles are not molarity

Kc needs mol/L. If a problem gives moles and a flask volume, divide every amount by the volume before you use the ICE table or the expression. Kp uses partial pressures, normally in atm.

ICE

Initial, Change, Equilibrium

Write the starting amounts, then the changes in terms of one unknown x, using the coefficients: reactants change by −(coefficient)x and products by +(coefficient)x. Each equilibrium entry is I + C.

One measurement

One number unlocks the table

Measuring a single equilibrium amount gives x, because I + C = E for that species. Then every other equilibrium amount follows. A measured total pressure works the same way for gases: add up the E row and set it equal to the total.

Direction

x can come out negative

If the measured product amount is smaller than its initial amount, x is negative: the reaction ran in reverse. The table still works. Q > K at the start is the reason.

Reporting

K is a plain number

K is normally written without units, and AP questions give it that way. Keep the significant figures of the data, and remember that pure solids and liquids appear in the ICE table only as "excess" and never in K.

4. Calculating K on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Write the K expression first, then substitute equilibrium values that match it term by term.
  • Show the moles-to-molarity conversion with units: "0.600 mol ÷ 2.00 L = 0.300 M."
  • Lay out the ICE table, or at least state x and how you got it from the measured value.
  • Use the coefficients in the change row: for 2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3, the changes are −2x, −x, +2x.
  • Report K as a number without units, with significant figures consistent with the data.
  • If asked whether a mixture is at equilibrium, calculate Q from the given values and compare with K.

Common mistakes

  • Plugging moles straight into Kc when the volume is not 1.00 L.
  • Using initial amounts in the K expression.
  • Forgetting the coefficients in the C row (writing −x for a species with coefficient 2).
  • Forgetting the exponents in the K expression after correctly finding the equilibrium amounts.
  • Including a solid in the ICE table's K calculation, or treating its "amount" as a concentration.
  • Assuming x is the equilibrium amount of a product. x is the change per unit coefficient; E = I + (coefficient)x.
