2. Key ideas

Definition

ΔG° decides favorability

ΔG° is the standard free energy change, for all reactants and products in standard states (pure solids and liquids, 1 M solutions, 1 atm gases). ΔG° < 0: thermodynamically favored. ΔG° > 0: not favored; the reverse process is favored.

Vocabulary

"Favored," not "spontaneous"

The course uses "thermodynamically favored" instead of "spontaneous," because spontaneous sounds like "fast" or "sudden." ΔG° says nothing about speed. A favored reaction can take millions of years (topic 9.4).

Two routes

Two ways to calculate ΔG°

From formation values: ΔG° = ΣΔG°f(products) − ΣΔG°f(reactants), using ΔG°f = 0 for elements in standard states. Or from ΔH° and ΔS°: ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°. Both are on the equations sheet, and at 298 K they agree within rounding.

Units

Convert ΔS° to kJ

ΔH° is in kJ/mol but ΔS° is in J/(mol·K). Divide ΔS° by 1000 before multiplying by T. Forgetting this makes TΔS° a thousand times too big, the most common lost point in this topic. T must be in kelvins.

Sign table

Two cases need no math

ΔH° < 0 and ΔS° > 0: both terms make ΔG° negative, favored at all T. ΔH° > 0 and ΔS° < 0: both make ΔG° positive, favored at no T. When the signs agree, temperature decides: high T favors the ΔS° > 0 case, low T favors the ΔH° < 0 case.

Crossover

Where ΔG° = 0

When ΔH° and ΔS° have the same sign, set ΔG° = 0 to find T = ΔH°/ΔS°. Above that temperature the entropy term dominates; below it the enthalpy term dominates. Freezing water (ΔH° < 0, ΔS° < 0) crosses at 273 K, which is why ice forms below 0 °C.

4. Free energy on the FRQ

What earns points

  • Write ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°, convert ΔS° to kJ/(mol·K), substitute with units, and give ΔG° in kJ/mol with its sign.
  • State the conclusion explicitly: "ΔG° < 0, so the reaction is thermodynamically favored at 298 K."
  • For "favored at all/no/high/low temperatures," cite both signs: "ΔH° < 0 and ΔS° > 0, so ΔG° < 0 at all T."
  • For a crossover, set ΔG° = 0 and solve T = ΔH°/ΔS°, with consistent units; say which side of it is favored.
  • When asked which factor drives a process, compare the sizes of ΔH° and TΔS°.
  • For ΣΔG°f, multiply by coefficients and use zero for elements in their standard states.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing J and kJ: ΔH° = −92.2 kJ/mol with ΔS° = −198.7 J/(mol·K) means TΔS° = 298 × (−0.1987) = −59.2 kJ/mol.
  • Using °C for T. At 25 °C, T = 298 K.
  • Saying "spontaneous means fast," or that ΔG° < 0 tells you the rate.
  • Claiming an endothermic process can never be favored. Dissolving NaNO₃ or NH₄NO₃ is endothermic and favored because of TΔS°.
  • Getting the subtraction backward: ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS°, so a positive ΔS° lowers ΔG°.
  • Setting S° = 0 for elements when computing ΔS° (only ΔH°f and ΔG°f are zero for elements).
