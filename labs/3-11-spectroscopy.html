2. Key ideas

Microwave

Rotational transitions

Microwave photons have the least energy of the three regions the AP exam tests. Absorbing one makes a polar molecule rotate faster: a jump to a higher rotational energy level. A microwave oven heats food by setting water molecules spinning.

Infrared

Vibrational transitions

Infrared photons carry more energy, enough to make bonds stretch and bend more strongly. Each kind of bond (O–H, C=O, C–H) absorbs at characteristic IR wavelengths, which is why IR spectra identify functional groups.

UV and visible

Electronic transitions

Ultraviolet and visible photons have enough energy to promote a valence electron to a higher energy level. Colored substances absorb some visible wavelengths; the color you see is made of the wavelengths that are not absorbed.

Energy order

Rotational < vibrational < electronic

The spacing between rotational levels is smallest, between vibrational levels larger, and between electronic levels largest. That is why the order of the regions (microwave, infrared, UV-visible) matches the order of the transitions.

Quantized

Only exact energies are absorbed

A molecule absorbs a photon only if the photon's energy matches the difference between two of its energy levels. That is what makes a spectrum a fingerprint: each substance has its own set of levels.

Beyond the three

X-rays and others

X-rays carry enough energy to remove core electrons (as in photoelectron spectroscopy, topic 1.6). Radio waves and gamma rays involve nuclear effects that are beyond the AP course.

4. Explaining it on the FRQ

What earns points on the FRQ

  • Name the region and the transition together: "infrared radiation causes transitions between vibrational energy levels."
  • Compare energies through wavelength or frequency: shorter λ, higher ν, higher E per photon.
  • Say that the photon's energy must equal the energy difference between the two levels.
  • Use E = hν and c = λν with SI units: wavelength in meters, frequency in s−1, energy in joules.
  • For color, say what is absorbed and what reaches your eye (the transmitted or reflected wavelengths).
  • Link IR absorption to specific bonds when a spectrum is given.

Common mistakes

  • Swapping IR and microwave: IR is vibrational, microwave is rotational.
  • Thinking longer wavelength means more energy. It is the opposite.
  • Saying a microwave oven "excites electrons." Its photons have about 105 times too little energy.
  • Saying a colored solution "is" the color it absorbs. A blue solution absorbs orange-red light.
  • Forgetting to convert nm to m (1 nm = 10−9 m) before using c = λν.
  • Treating "brighter light" as "higher-energy photons." Intensity is the number of photons, not the energy of each.
