Topic 2.1

Types of Chemical Bonds

Open lab → Topic 2.2

Intramolecular Force and Potential Energy

Open lab → Topic 2.3

Structure of Ionic Solids

Open lab → Topic 2.6

Open lab → Topic 2.7

VSEPR and Hybridization

Spin 3D models of every VSEPR shape from linear to octahedral, add lone pairs and watch the molecular geometry change, and read off the bond angles, hybridization, polarity, and sigma and pi bonds for 16 real molecules.

Intermolecular and Interparticle Forces

Open lab → Topic 3.2

Properties of Solids

Run melting, hammer, conductivity, and solubility tests on mystery solids, classify each one as ionic, molecular, covalent network, or metallic, and stress, melt, and electrify a particle model to see why each type behaves as it does.

Open lab → Topic 3.3

Solids, Liquids, and Gases

Open lab → Topic 3.4

Ideal Gas Law

Open lab → Topic 3.5

Kinetic Molecular Theory

Open lab → Topic 3.6

Deviation from Ideal Gas Law

Open lab → Topic 3.7

Solutions and Mixtures

Open lab → Topic 3.8

Open lab → Topic 3.9

Open lab → Topic 3.10

Solubility

Open lab → Topic 3.11

Spectroscopy and the Electromagnetic Spectrum

Sweep across the electromagnetic spectrum and watch a water molecule rotate, vibrate, or have an electron promoted, with the photon energy and the matching energy-level jump calculated at each wavelength.

Open lab → Topic 3.12

Properties of Photons

Open lab → Topic 3.13

Beer–Lambert Law

Unit 5 Kinetics

Topic 5.1

Reaction Rates

Watch reactant and product concentrations change over time, read average and instantaneous rates off the graph, use coefficients to relate the rates of every species, and test how concentration, temperature, surface area, and a catalyst speed a reaction up.

Open lab → Topic 5.2

Introduction to Rate Law

Run initial-rate trials at concentrations you choose, compare pairs of trials to find the order in each reactant, and test your rate law by checking that every trial gives the same rate constant with the right units.

Open lab → Topic 5.3

Concentration Changes Over Time

Plot concentration, ln of concentration, and 1/concentration against time to find which graph is linear, read the rate constant from its slope, use the integrated rate laws, and see why only a first-order reaction has a constant half-life.

Open lab → Topic 5.4

Elementary Reactions

Build elementary steps from colliding particles, read each step's rate law straight from its coefficients, count possible collision partners in a particle box to see why, and learn when the coefficient shortcut does not work.

Open lab → Topic 5.5

Collision Model

Run a particle simulator where molecules must collide with enough energy and the right orientation to react, change temperature, concentration, and activation energy, and connect what you see to the shaded area of a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution.

Open lab → Topic 5.6

Reaction Energy Profile

Shape the energy profile of an elementary reaction, read off the activation energy, ΔH and the reverse barrier, walk the atoms through the transition state, and see why heating speeds up a reaction.

Open lab → Topic 5.7

Introduction to Reaction Mechanisms

Add up the elementary steps of real mechanisms, watch species cancel, label each one as reactant, product, intermediate or catalyst, and see an intermediate rise and vanish in a simulated reaction.

Open lab → Topic 5.8

Reaction Mechanism and Rate Law

Run initial-rate trials on a simulated reaction, work out the experimental rate law, and test which proposed mechanism's slow step predicts it.

Open lab → Topic 5.9

Pre-Equilibrium Approximation

Derive rate laws step by step for mechanisms with a fast, reversible first step, then speed up the slow step to see when the pre-equilibrium approximation holds and when it breaks.

Open lab → Topic 5.10

Multistep Reaction Energy Profile

Build the energy profile of a two- or three-step mechanism from each step's activation energy and ΔH, find the intermediates and the rate-determining step, and test yourself on reading the peaks and valleys.

Open lab → Topic 5.11

Catalysis

Compare catalyzed and uncatalyzed energy profiles, see how a lower barrier lets far more collisions succeed, and trace the catalyst through homogeneous, acid, surface and enzyme mechanisms as it is used and regenerated.

Unit 7 Equilibrium

Topic 7.1

Introduction to Equilibrium

Seal N₂O₄ and NO₂ in a flask, run the clock, and watch the particles, the concentration graph, and the forward and reverse rates settle into a dynamic equilibrium.

Open lab → Topic 7.2

Direction of Reversible Reactions

Set the amounts of H₂, I₂, and HI, race the forward rate against the reverse rate to see which way the reaction goes, then read rate and concentration graphs to call the direction yourself.

Open lab → Topic 7.3

Reaction Quotient and Equilibrium Constant

Write Kc and Kp expressions for gas, aqueous, and heterogeneous reactions, leave out solids and liquids, plug in current amounts to get Q, and see where Q sits relative to K on a log scale.

Open lab → Topic 7.4

Calculating the Equilibrium Constant

Turn measured equilibrium data into K: convert moles to molarity, build an ICE table from one measured amount or a total pressure, substitute into Kc or Kp, and check your own answer.

Open lab → Topic 7.5

Magnitude of the Equilibrium Constant

Turn a K dial from 10⁻¹⁵ to 10¹⁵ and watch the equilibrium mixture swing from all reactant to all product, compare real reactions such as water's autoionization and neutralization, and sort reactions by what their K says.

Open lab → Topic 7.6

Properties of the Equilibrium Constant

Reverse, scale, and add given reactions until they match a target equation, and watch K follow along: inverted, raised to a power, and multiplied, with every step shown on a log-scale ledger.

Open lab → Topic 7.7

Calculating Equilibrium Concentrations

Start from any initial mixture and a value of K, use Q to find the direction, build the ICE table, and solve for the equilibrium amounts with a perfect square, the small-x approximation, or the quadratic formula.

Open lab → Topic 7.8

Representations of Equilibrium

Watch a box of particles react toward equilibrium, read the same story in a concentration–time and a rate–time graph, calculate Q from particle counts, and pick out which diagram shows equilibrium.

Open lab → Topic 7.9

Introduction to Le Châtelier’s Principle

Stress six classic equilibrium systems by adding or removing a species, heating or cooling, compressing, diluting, or adding a catalyst, and watch the shift in color, pH and concentration.

Open lab → Topic 7.10

Reaction Quotient and Le Châtelier’s Principle

Disturb an equilibrium, calculate Q the instant after the change, compare it with K on a log number line, and solve for the new equilibrium; see why concentration and volume changes move Q while temperature moves K.

Open lab → Topic 7.11

Introduction to Solubility Equilibria

Convert between Ksp and molar solubility for thirteen salts with every step shown, see on a log plot why Ksp only ranks salts of the same ion ratio, and predict whether mixing two solutions makes a precipitate.

Open lab → Topic 7.12

Introduction to Acids and Bases

Open lab → Topic 8.5

Open lab → Topic 8.8

Open lab → Topic 8.11

pH and Solubility

Unit 9 Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry

7–9% of the multiple-choice section · Unit 9 terms
Topic 9.1

Introduction to Entropy

Watch particles before and after melting, boiling, gas expansion, heating, and reactions, predict the sign of ΔS for each, and see how matter and energy dispersal set the answer.

Open lab → Topic 9.2

Absolute Entropy and Entropy Change

Build ΔS° for a reaction from standard molar entropies on a stacked-bar entropy ledger, reverse or scale the equation, switch water between liquid and gas, and see the classic calculation mistakes side by side.

Open lab → Topic 9.3

Gibbs Free Energy and Thermodynamic Favorability

Set ΔH°, ΔS°, and temperature, watch ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS° move on a live graph and a four-quadrant sign map, find the crossover temperature, and check the result against free energies of formation.

Open lab → Topic 9.4

Thermodynamic and Kinetic Control

Set ΔG°, the activation barrier, temperature, a catalyst, and how long you wait, then watch whether a thermodynamically favored reaction actually happens or sits under kinetic control.

Open lab → Topic 9.5

Free Energy and Equilibrium

Convert between ΔG° and K in either direction with a live graph, a worked calculation, and an equilibrium-mixture bar, using real reactions from ammonia synthesis to the autoionization of water.

Open lab → Topic 9.6

Free Energy of Dissolution

Step a salt through dissolving at the particle level, see the sign of the enthalpy and entropy change for each stage, and compare ΔH°, TΔS°, and ΔG° for six real salts as the temperature changes.

Open lab → Topic 9.7

Coupled Reactions

Drive an uphill reaction: couple it to ATP hydrolysis or CO combustion, or supply electrical or light energy, and watch the free-energy ledger decide whether the overall process is thermodynamically favored.

Open lab → Topic 9.8

Galvanic (Voltaic) and Electrolytic Cells

Build an electrochemical cell from two half-cells, run it as a galvanic or an electrolytic cell, and watch electrons in the wire, ions in the salt bridge, electrode masses and gas bubbles respond.

Open lab → Topic 9.9

Cell Potential and Free Energy

Pick any two half-reactions from a standard reduction potential ladder and get the balanced cell reaction, E°cell, n, ΔG° = −nFE° and K worked out step by step, with the sign telling you whether the cell is favored.

Open lab → Topic 9.10

Cell Potential Under Nonstandard Conditions

Change the ion concentrations in a galvanic or concentration cell, watch Q move the cell potential above or below E° on a live graph, and run the cell down until it reaches equilibrium at E = 0.

Open lab → Topic 9.11

Electrolysis and Faraday’s Law

Run an electroplating bench: set the current and time, follow charge to moles of electrons to moles and grams of metal (or liters of gas), work backward to the time or the ion's charge, and solve randomized Faraday challenges.
