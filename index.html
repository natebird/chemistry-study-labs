Unit 2 Compound Structure and Properties

7–9% of the multiple-choice section · Unit 2 terms
Topic 2.1

Types of Chemical Bonds

Pick any two elements on a periodic table to see whether they form an ionic, polar covalent, nonpolar covalent, or metallic bond, how the electrons are shared or transferred, and why, then race through a which-bond-is-more-polar drill.

Open lab → Topic 2.2

Intramolecular Force and Potential Energy

Drag two atoms together along a potential energy curve to find the bond length and bond energy, compare curves for single, double, and triple bonds and for small and large atoms, then use Coulomb's law to rank the attraction between ion pairs.

Open lab → Topic 2.3

Structure of Ionic Solids

Rotate a 3D rock-salt crystal, slide one layer of ions past another until the crystal shatters, melt or dissolve the solid to see when it conducts electricity, and use Coulomb's law to rank lattice energies.

Open lab → Topic 2.4

Structure of Metals and Alloys

Build pure metals, substitutional alloys like brass, and interstitial alloys like steel atom by atom, then push the layers, switch on a voltage, and see why metals bend and conduct and why steel is stiffer than iron.

Open lab → Topic 2.5

Lewis Diagrams

Draw Lewis diagrams for 22 molecules and ions by clicking bonds and atoms, get instant feedback on electron count and octets, or step through the counting procedure one move at a time, including incomplete and expanded octets.

Open lab → Topic 2.6

Resonance and Formal Charge

Flip between resonance structures of ozone, nitrate, carbonate, and benzene and see the resonance hybrid they average to, then use a live formal-charge calculator to pick the best Lewis diagram for CO2, OCN-, and N2O and see where the Lewis model breaks down.

Open lab → Topic 2.7

VSEPR and Hybridization

Spin 3D models of every VSEPR shape from linear to octahedral, add lone pairs and watch the molecular geometry change, and read off the bond angles, hybridization, polarity, and sigma and pi bonds for 16 real molecules.

Unit 3 Properties of Substances and Mixtures

Topic 3.1

Intermolecular and Interparticle Forces

Inspect pairs of molecules to see which intermolecular forces act between them, then rank real boiling points for classic AP comparison sets and check your reasoning.

Open lab → Topic 3.2

Properties of Solids

Run melting, hammer, conductivity, and solubility tests on mystery solids, classify each one as ionic, molecular, covalent network, or metallic, and stress, melt, and electrify a particle model to see why each type behaves as it does.

Open lab → Topic 3.3

Solids, Liquids, and Gases

Heat and cool water, ethanol, and diethyl ether in a particle-level model to see how solids, liquids, and gases differ, then compare their vapor pressures in a sealed flask and connect them to intermolecular forces.

Open lab → Topic 3.4

Ideal Gas Law

Drive a gas cylinder with sliders for moles, temperature, volume, and pressure, watch the particles respond, and see every PV = nRT and Dalton's-law calculation worked out step by step.

Open lab → Topic 3.5

Kinetic Molecular Theory

Compare Maxwell–Boltzmann speed distributions for two gas samples as you change temperature and molar mass, watch the particles move, and see why average kinetic energy depends only on kelvin temperature.

Open lab → Topic 3.6

Deviation from Ideal Gas Law

Push real gases to high pressure and low temperature, watch PV/nRT move away from 1, and see at the particle level whether molecular volume or intermolecular attractions cause the deviation.

Open lab → Topic 3.7

Solutions and Mixtures

Weigh a solid into a volumetric flask or dilute a stock solution, and watch the molarity, ion concentrations and particle picture update step by step.

Open lab → Topic 3.8

Representations of Solutions

Build particle diagrams of dissolved ionic and molecular solutes with correctly oriented water molecules, then find the mistakes in eight flawed diagrams.

Open lab → Topic 3.9

Separation of Solutions and Mixtures

Run a chromatography plate, change the solvent, measure Rf values, and distill two-liquid mixtures to see how differences in intermolecular forces separate the parts of a mixture.

Open lab → Topic 3.10

Solubility

Predict whether ten solutes dissolve in water or hexane, check each prediction against an IMF scorecard, and watch alcohol solubility fall as the carbon chain grows.

Open lab → Topic 3.11

Spectroscopy and the Electromagnetic Spectrum

Sweep across the electromagnetic spectrum and watch a water molecule rotate, vibrate, or have an electron promoted, with the photon energy and the matching energy-level jump calculated at each wavelength.

Open lab → Topic 3.12

Properties of Photons

Move an electron between hydrogen energy levels to emit or absorb photons, calculate each photon's energy, frequency and wavelength with E = hν and c = λν, and shine light on a metal to see why photon energy, not brightness, decides whether electrons come out.

Open lab → Topic 3.13

Beer–Lambert Law

Drive a spectrophotometer with A = εbc, build a calibration curve to find an unknown concentration, and test how classic lab errors such as fingerprints, a wet cuvette or the wrong wavelength change the result.

Unit 6 Thermochemistry

7–9% of the multiple-choice section · Unit 6 terms
Topic 6.1

Endothermic and Exothermic Processes

Dissolve salts in a cup of water, read the thermometer, and decide whether each process is endothermic or exothermic, then explain why in terms of system, surroundings, and attractions between particles.

Open lab → Topic 6.2

Energy Diagrams

Build energy diagrams for reactions and phase changes to scale, reverse them, track where the energy goes, and practice reading ΔH off unlabeled diagrams.

Open lab → Topic 6.3

Heat Transfer and Thermal Equilibrium

Put a hot body and a cold body in contact, watch particle collisions carry energy across the boundary, and follow both temperatures until they meet at thermal equilibrium.

Open lab → Topic 6.4

Heat Capacity and Calorimetry

Run coffee-cup calorimetry experiments: drop hot metal into water to find its specific heat, dissolve salts and neutralize acids to find ΔH, and see how heat loss to the room skews the results.

Open lab → Topic 6.5

Energy of Phase Changes

Heat ice into steam or cool steam into ice along a live heating curve, watch the particles change phase, and calculate the energy of every sloped and flat segment with q = mcΔT and q = nΔH.

Open lab → Topic 6.6

Introduction to Enthalpy of Reaction

Pick a reaction and an amount, then watch the energy diagram, the heat flow, and a water bath thermometer respond as every q = n × ΔH step is worked out, including per-mole-of-reaction scaling.

Open lab → Topic 6.7

Bond Enthalpies

Break every reactant bond, form every product bond, and watch the energy staircase estimate ΔH from average bond enthalpies, with editable bond values and a bonds-that-change shortcut.

Open lab → Topic 6.8

Enthalpy of Formation

Route a reaction through its elements on an enthalpy diagram, compute ΔH°rxn = ΣΔH°f(products) − ΣΔH°f(reactants) step by step, solve for a missing ΔH°f, and see what each classic mistake does to the answer.

Open lab → Topic 6.9

Hess’s Law

Solve Hess's law puzzles by reversing and scaling step equations until they add to the target, watching species cancel and the ΔH values add up on a step-by-step energy path.
